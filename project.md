# The project

## Research question

**How can Extended Reality (XR) technologies be integrated into SMEs' design processes to improve ergonomic validation and system reconfigurability, thereby enhancing innovation, productivity and workplace conditions?**

## Scientific ambition

HUMANIRE explores a human-centred approach to the design of industrial systems in the context of Industry 5.0.

XR technologies — including virtual, augmented and mixed reality — make it possible to simulate industrial situations and human-machine interactions before a physical system is implemented. The project focuses on making these technologies usable as part of established industrial design processes.

A particular challenge is the interoperability between XR environments and the digital tools already used by industrial companies, including CAD, PLM and digital-twin technologies.

## Three research challenges

### 1. Integrating XR into industrial design

Develop a reusable methodology for incorporating XR simulation and validation into the design and reconfiguration of production systems.

### 2. Connecting industrial data to XR

Define methods and protocols for transferring relevant information from CAD, PLM and digital-twin environments into XR applications.

### 3. Making ergonomics part of the design loop

Combine ergonomic assessment, human-centred design and XR so that ergonomic considerations can be addressed iteratively during system design.

## Expected contributions

The project is expected to contribute:

- an interoperability model for CAD/digital-twin to XR data pipelines;
- validated ergonomic assessment protocols combining XR, motion capture and established ergonomic methods;
- a user-centred framework combining production-system design and ergonomics;
- knowledge about barriers to XR adoption in SMEs;
- two industrial proofs of concept demonstrating the approach.

## Approach

The project follows an iterative **develop → experiment → analyse** approach. Scientific developments are progressively confronted with industrial requirements and experimental results.

The work is organised into six complementary work packages.
